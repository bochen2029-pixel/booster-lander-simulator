01
THREE RINGS, ONE JOB
The stable member must not move. Three gimbals — the outer drum about the vehicle X axis, the middle frame about Z, the inner (the platform itself) about Y — absorb every rotation the vehicle makes. The IRIG gyros feel any motion of the platform and the servos drive the gimbals until it stops.
RUN STEP TEST
PTC ROLL
02
GIMBAL LOCK
Take the middle gimbal to ±90° and the inner and outer axes become the same axis. The platform loses a degree of freedom and the required ring rates go to infinity — the servos cannot follow and the reference is lost. The IRIG floats hit their stops, the reference is physically lost and NO ATT stays lit until you re-align. In FLY, yaw the vehicle until the red cap reaches the reticle — then try to fly around it instead. Apollo lit a caution lamp at 70° and the computer forced coarse align at 85°.

03
THE SERVO LOOP
Gyro float angles are resolved through the IGA and MGA resolvers into torque-motor commands; each motor loop is the second-order, accel-limited servo straight out of p1_gimbal.c:
Δo = −(θx·cos i + θz·sin i) / cos m
Δm = −(θz·cos i − θx·sin i)
Δi = −θy + sin m · Δo
gacc = ωn²·Δ − 2ζωn·ġ ; g += dt·ġ ; ġ += dt·gacc
ωn={{ v.wn }} rad/s ζ={{ v.zeta }} lim={{ v.acc }}°/s² · sec m → ∞ at lock
Detune ωn in SETUP and watch the float angles and platform error grow: the gimbals lag the vehicle and the stable member is dragged off its reference.
04
RATE WINDUP AT THE STOPS
Clamp the gimbal position at a mechanical stop but leave the rate state alone and the servo keeps integrating a phantom rate. On reversal that rate must unwind first — the ring sits still, then lurches. Run the A/B and read the lash.

05
WHAT THE PIPAs COUNT
Three accelerometers on the platform integrate specific force into velocity, one pulse per 5.85 cm/s. Sitting on a desk they still count — that is gravity. Your phone's accelerometer is standing in for them; DATA shows the pulse totals in stable-member axes.
